# Road junction blend review (read-only)

**Disposition: HOLD.** This is a conceptual review of the current `src/road-draping.ts` snapshot plus the saved `road-fix/diagnostics` artifacts. It does not validate the later evolving candidate, and it is not an ACCEPT decision. No source files were changed.

## What the code does

`reconcileJunctions` creates junction events for intersecting centerline segments; for endpoint/T/rounded joins it also accepts a closest-pair fallback when the distance is at most half the sum of road widths. It clusters event centers within 0.25 m subject to a bounded cluster extent, selects one priority group for the target grade plane, and blends a cosine falloff over the pavement/nearby profile groups. The blend changes only Y; input XZ and the source DEM are preserved. Bridges are skipped. This is a real geometry transform, not a driving-normal clamp.

The compressed-edge branch evaluates falloff per pavement vertex, while regular groups share a centerline-derived weight across the cross-section. That is sensible for tight bends where adjacent pavement rows nearly coincide in XZ, but it makes the two branches worth testing at their boundaries and across overlapping planes. The selection and blending are order-independent with respect to mutating source profiles because targets use a saved `original` position set.

## Evidence and interpretation

The currently saved `outputs/village-fidelity-20261004/road-fix/diagnostics/comparison.json` does **not** contain the larger cross-slope regression quoted in the review request; its current saved values are different. For ROAD, `renderedCrossSlope` p99/max are 0.0893/0.2657 before and 0.0922/0.2657 after. For TRACK they are 0.2000/0.2689 both before and after. ROAD rendered pavement-triangle grade improves at its extreme (max 5.1995 to 0.7035), while p95/p99 rise from 0.1404/0.2113 to 0.1556/0.2449. The ROAD p99 increase is therefore present in the saved artifact, while the cited TRACK cross-slope max 0.5818 and ROAD p99/max 0.152/0.346 are not reproducible from this particular file. Obtain a fresh, atomically paired before/after diagnostic run for the final candidate before resolving that discrepancy.

The saved rendered-cross-slope top samples are useful for cause classification: the ROAD worst value 0.2657 occurs on ordinary pavement for ID `310174515`, with no skirt, bridge, owner transition, missing centerline, or class mismatch. `sourceBranchCrossSlope`, `ownFacetBank`, and independent lateral `crossSectionSlope` agree closely (~0.266, ~0.266, ~0.264), so that sample is a genuine crossfall in the rendered pavement rather than merely a source-vs-facet tangent projection or a skirt artifact. In contrast, separate `rendered-before.json` skirt maxima include cut-skirt contacts with near-vertical normals and source-tangent cross slopes around 5.9 while their lateral cross-section estimate is only about 0.22; such skirt rows must be excluded from pavement crossfall gates.

The ROAD p95/p99 pavement-grade shifts are broad-tail regressions, not explainable by the single maximum outlier being fixed. They need localized comparison of before/after triangle and vertex neighborhoods. Potential causes include the new profile/camber geometry, triangles spanning a bend or junction field gradient, and mesh sampling/triangulation. The existing 1 m contact samples alone cannot distinguish these. The independently computed triangle grades make the regression geometric in the rendered mesh, though attribution to the junction blend specifically is not established by this aggregate report.

## No-regression checks before ACCEPT

1. Regenerate before/after artifacts from the same source/data/runtime and include source revision/hash and timestamps in both. Resolve the mismatch between the requested reported cross-slope numbers and the current saved comparison file.
2. For ROAD p95/p99 and each reported cross-slope increase, report matched contact/triangle samples with road ID, chainage, XZ, class/owner, role (pavement vs skirt), triangle index, and local before/after vertex heights. Keep terrain/raw grade, rendered pavement grade, rendered crossfall, and driving normals as separate measures.
3. For changed crossfall samples, compare projection on the source road tangent, the rendered facet tangent, and an independent left/right pavement cross-section. Classify skirt, owner/class transition, bridge, missing lateral sample, and near-vertical contacts explicitly. Do not apply contact-normal bank caps as geometry evidence.
4. For the TRACK 0.5818 maximum cited by the parent, identify the exact triangle/road station and inspect the local mesh/capture. A maximum alone cannot establish whether it is an isolated sampling artifact or visible/driveable geometry.
5. Test true crossings, T-junctions, near-but-disconnected centerlines, grade-separated bridges, overlapping falloff fields, and the transition between shared-centerline and compressed-edge weighting. Specifically verify that the width-based closest-pair fallback does not create a false join and that nearby junction planes do not flatten intervening road sections.
6. Keep gates on the actual rendered pavement and raw DEM; preserve the current dramatic ROAD max-grade improvement, but do not accept worsening tail distributions without a documented threshold and explicit visual/physics rationale. Re-run road geometry and gameplay/contact checks on the final source, then compare same-camera actual-game captures.
